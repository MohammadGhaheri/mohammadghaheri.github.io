## Why I created BI++

There is an old saying in business: *profit is made when you buy well, not only when you sell at a higher price.*

In a modern company, buying well is also about **understanding and managing costs**. Which expense is increasing? Which product is actually profitable? Where are resources being wasted? Which action would make the biggest difference?

Answers to these questions may be scattered across sales spreadsheets, purchasing records, financial systems, and operational data.

But having the data is not enough. Even a beautifully designed chart is not necessarily useful.

**Data analysis creates business value when scattered information becomes reliable insight and, ultimately, better decisions.**

That is the philosophy behind BI++: learn Power BI as a way to solve business problems, while using AI tools to reduce the friction of learning, coding, troubleshooting, and exploration.

## How is BI++ different from a conventional Power BI course?

A typical software tutorial introduces a feature, demonstrates it, and moves on. That approach helps us learn an interface, but real analytical work is rarely a sequence of isolated buttons.

In BI++, I bring three capabilities together:

1. **Technical skills:** Power BI, Power Query, data modeling, DAX, and visual reporting.
2. **Analytical thinking:** defining business questions, selecting meaningful KPIs, and interpreting results.
3. **Working effectively with AI:** using an assistant to learn faster, explore solutions, draft code, and investigate errors.

The goal is not to outsource judgment to a chatbot. It is to spend less time on repetitive friction and more time asking the right questions.

## What will you learn in the BI++ course?

The lessons follow the practical path from raw data to an analytical report.

### 1. Data analytics and business intelligence foundations

We begin with the role of the data analyst, what business intelligence is, and why the distinction between data, information, and insight matters. Learners get familiar with Power BI and work through an early, compact project to understand the end-to-end workflow.

### 2. Connecting to data sources

We work with files, databases, and online sources, including Google Forms and Google Sheets. Python examples introduce reading data and calling a web service or API as another way to bring information into an analytical workflow.

### 3. Preparing data with Power Query

Real datasets rarely arrive ready for analysis. We explore data types, Applied Steps, error handling, and combining and transforming data in Power Query. AI tools are also used to suggest M code and help investigate problems—always with an emphasis on verifying their output.

### 4. Building a trustworthy data model

This part covers fact and dimension tables, relationships, star schema, context, and date modeling. The course also addresses Jalali (Persian) calendar needs and introduces model inspection with tools such as Tabular Editor and an AI assistant.

### 5. Writing analytical calculations with DAX

**DAX stands for Data Analysis Expressions.** It is the expression language used for analytical calculations in Power BI. Instead of relying only on default aggregations, we introduce how measures help answer business questions about sales, ratios, and performance.

Understanding **calculation context** is more valuable than simply memorizing function names.

### 6. Creating interactive reports and dashboards

We work with bar and column charts, line and area charts, pie charts, cards, KPIs, and map visuals. We then use filters, slicers, bookmarks, and visual interactions so people can explore data instead of viewing a static collection of charts.

### 7. Python visuals and sales forecasting

A practical example starts with a simple Python visual. It then develops a six-month linear sales forecast into a 12-month forecast that incorporates seasonal patterns.

This is an **educational forecasting exercise**, not a guarantee of future sales. Its purpose is to show how Python can extend the analytical possibilities of Power BI.

## Where does artificial intelligence fit into the process?

AI is not an isolated bonus lesson added to the end of BI++. It is part of how we approach several stages of the learning process.

For example, an AI assistant can help us:

- Understand unfamiliar concepts in plain language.
- Draft and explain M or Python code.
- Investigate transformation errors and potential model issues.
- Compare different approaches to the same analytical problem.
- Formulate more useful questions about a dataset.

The course introduces **Hella**, my Power BI AI assistant, and discusses opportunities and limitations around tools such as Copilot.

The guiding rule is simple: **AI-generated answers must be checked against the data model, business logic, and actual results.** A plausible formula can still produce misleading numbers when the data relationships or calculation context are wrong.

## Who should take BI++?

BI++ is designed for people who want to begin learning business intelligence, as well as Excel users and professionals who want to move beyond manual reporting.

It is not limited to people with the job title *Data Analyst*. Professionals in sales, finance, marketing, HR, operations, and planning can use data analysis to ask better questions in their own fields.

You do not need to be an experienced Python programmer to start. Curiosity, a willingness to practice, and some familiarity with tabular data are useful foundations.

## What should you be able to do after learning the material?

The course is designed to build an understanding of the complete analytical workflow:

**Raw Data → Clean Data → Data Model → DAX → Visualization → Insight → Decision**

In practical terms, that means knowing how to identify and prepare data sources, build a reliable model, define analytical calculations, and create interactive reports that answer meaningful questions.

A dashboard's appearance matters, but **a beautiful dashboard with unreliable calculations is still an unreliable dashboard**.

## Frequently asked questions about BI++

### Is BI++ the same thing as Power BI?

No. **Power BI** is Microsoft's business intelligence and reporting platform. **BI++** is the name of my training course, which combines Power BI education with practical AI-assisted workflows.

### Do I need Python experience before taking the course?

No prior Python programming experience is required to begin. The course introduces installation and basic scripts before using Python for data and visual examples.

### Does BI++ include DAX?

Yes. DAX is part of the Power BI analytical calculation workflow covered in the course. The emphasis is on understanding the purpose of measures and the logic of calculations. Learners who want much deeper DAX specialization can continue with dedicated training.

### Can AI replace Power BI or a data analyst?

AI can help explain concepts, suggest code, and troubleshoot problems. It cannot eliminate the need to define the business question, assess data quality, validate calculations, or take responsibility for decisions. BI++ treats AI as a capable assistant, not an infallible analyst.
